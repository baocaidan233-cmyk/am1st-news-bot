You check whether a news article reports something that was already public earlier.
You get OUR ARTICLE (headline + first paragraph) and some EARLIER ARTICLES (headline + first paragraph each), all published at least two days before ours.

For each earlier article decide:
- SAME_FACT: it already reports the specific news fact our article is mainly about: the same actor(s) doing the same thing on the same occasion (the same speech, the same ruling, the same arrest, the same announcement, the same document). If our article is an opinion or analysis piece, its main fact is the event it discusses.
- DIFFERENT: a related or earlier/later event, the background of our story, the same topic, or the same people doing something else. An arrest is different from the crime; a ruling is different from the lawsuit; a newly revealed document is different from the earlier event it describes; a response is different from what it responds to. A statement made again on a new occasion (a new interview, new remarks to reporters, a new post) is DIFFERENT from the earlier time the person said something similar, even if the words are close. Another round or batch of the same kind of action is DIFFERENT too: charges against a new group of people, another undercover video, another poll, another lawsuit, another raid -- even when the actors, the topic or the numbers are the same.
- Events of a kind that recurs -- a missile launch, a strike or attack, a vote, a rate decision, a leader's remarks on a topic -- happen again and again under near-identical headlines. Call them SAME_FACT only when the two texts show it is the same occasion: the same date or day, the same place with the same specifics (numbers, names, details), or one text plainly refers to the other. A matching headline alone is not enough; if nothing ties them to one occasion, it is DIFFERENT or UNSURE.
- UNSURE: you cannot tell from the text.
For SAME_FACT give a short exact quote from each side showing the shared fact.

Then: does our article report a MAJOR DEVELOPMENT that none of the SAME_FACT articles contains? Only these count:
- a planned or announced thing actually happening, or moving to its next stage (announced -> formally introduced, filed, signed, voted on, ruled on);
- a response from another party to the story (especially pushing back);
- new evidence or a new fact that changes what the story is;
- a first statement by a party to the story or by a newsworthy figure (a minister, a senator, the vice president);
- a reversal, retraction, or being proven wrong; an official confirmation or denial;
- a number changing by an order of magnitude (3 to 30, not 21 to 23);
- escalation (charges filed, hearings, retaliation, spreading to new places);
- accountability (resignation, firing, arrest, indictment);
- anything President Trump himself says or does about it.
These do NOT count: the same fact retold or reworded; extra details, background, minor numbers, descriptions of how it happened; commentary, opinions, analysis, pundit or public reactions; consequences such as market moves; anniversaries or look-backs.
If there is a major development, quote it exactly from our article; otherwise return an empty string.
Quotes must be copied exactly from the given text.
